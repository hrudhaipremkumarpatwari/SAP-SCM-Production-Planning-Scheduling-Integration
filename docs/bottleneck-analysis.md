# Bottleneck Analysis and Mitigation

The Week 3 scenario places peak-week demand close to the packaging resource limit so that capacity constraints can be evaluated realistically.

## Main bottleneck

The packaging line is treated as the most constrained shared resource. In the illustrative scenario, planned production of about 138.5 tonnes against 145 tonnes of weekly packaging capacity gives utilization of approximately 95.5%.

## Risks

High utilization can increase the chance of:

- late orders when equipment stops unexpectedly
- production queues after long changeovers
- material shortages disrupting the sequence
- urgent demand forcing frequent rescheduling
- reduced recovery time after a delay

## Mitigation options

1. Sequence similar products together to reduce changeover loss.
2. Reserve limited capacity for high-priority or urgent orders.
3. Use overtime only when the service benefit justifies the extra cost.
4. Split large lots when doing so improves schedule feasibility.
5. Expedite or use alternate sourcing for critical packaging materials.
6. Reschedule lower-priority orders when a high-priority shortage occurs.
7. Monitor finite-capacity exceptions daily during peak periods.

## Scenario responses

### Equipment breakdown
Resequence unaffected operations, use available buffer capacity, evaluate overtime and protect priority customer requirements.

### Packaging-material delay
Use alternate source or expedited supply where feasible, resequence products with available material and avoid starting orders that cannot be completed.

### Demand spike
Recheck available capacity, prioritize confirmed customer demand, split or shift lots and communicate any unavoidable delivery impact.
